# DocuMind Company Handbook

Welcome to DocuMind. This handbook outlines our company policies, culture, and expectations for all team members. Please read it carefully and refer back to it whenever you have questions about how we operate.

## Our Mission and Values

DocuMind exists to make organizational knowledge accessible and actionable through artificial intelligence. We believe that the most valuable resource in any company is the collective knowledge of its people, and too often that knowledge is locked away in documents that nobody reads, wikis that nobody updates, and conversations that are quickly forgotten.

Our core values guide everything we do:

**Transparency First** — We share information openly across the organization. Default to public channels, document decisions, and explain the reasoning behind changes. If information is not explicitly confidential, it should be accessible to everyone.

**Ship and Iterate** — We favor shipping early and iterating based on real feedback over perfecting in isolation. A working prototype that reaches users teaches us more than months of theoretical planning. We measure success by impact delivered, not hours worked.

**Respect the Craft** — We take pride in the quality of our work. Code should be well-tested, documentation should be clear, and user experiences should be thoughtful. We review each other's work not to gatekeep, but to learn and improve together.

**Customer Empathy** — Every team member, regardless of role, should understand our customers' problems. We regularly participate in customer calls, read support tickets, and use our own product daily.

## Team Structure and Roles

DocuMind operates with a flat organizational structure organized around cross-functional squads. Each squad owns a specific area of the product and operates with significant autonomy.

**Engineering Squad** — Responsible for the core platform, API infrastructure, search algorithms, and AI integration. The squad includes backend engineers, frontend engineers, and ML engineers who work closely together on feature development.

**Product Squad** — Drives the product roadmap, conducts user research, designs interfaces, and defines success metrics. Product managers and designers work directly within engineering squads during active development cycles.

**Growth Squad** — Handles marketing, developer relations, community building, and partnership development. This squad is responsible for user acquisition, activation, and retention metrics.

**Operations Squad** — Manages finance, legal compliance, HR operations, and office administration. This team ensures the company runs smoothly so everyone else can focus on building great products.

Each squad has a lead who is responsible for coordination and communication but does not manage people in a traditional hierarchy. Career growth and performance feedback are handled through a mentorship system where each team member chooses a mentor from the senior staff.

## Onboarding Process

New team members go through a structured two-week onboarding program designed to get them productive and connected as quickly as possible.

**Week One** focuses on environment setup, tool access, and understanding the product. New hires will set up their development environment on day one with help from an assigned onboarding buddy. They will complete a guided tour of the codebase, review the architecture documentation, and deploy their first change to a staging environment by the end of the week. Each day includes a one-hour session with a different team member to learn about their area of responsibility.

**Week Two** shifts to active contribution. New hires pick up a starter task from the backlog, participate in their first sprint planning, and join customer calls to hear firsthand how people use DocuMind. By the end of week two, they should have shipped at least one small feature or bug fix to production.

All new hires receive a welcome kit that includes a company laptop configured with our standard development tools, a DocuMind branded notebook, and a reading list of key documents and blog posts about our industry and technology stack.

## Remote Work Guidelines

DocuMind is a remote-first company. Team members can work from anywhere, and we do not require physical presence in any office location. However, remote work comes with expectations around communication and availability.

**Core Hours** — All team members should be available for synchronous communication between 10:00 AM and 2:00 PM Eastern Time on weekdays. Outside these hours, asynchronous communication is the default. Respond to messages within 24 hours during workdays.

**Video Calls** — Keep your camera on during team meetings unless you have a specific reason not to. This helps build connection and trust. One-on-one calls can be audio-only if both parties prefer.

**Home Office Setup** — The company provides a $1,500 stipend for home office equipment including a desk, chair, monitor, and peripherals. This stipend is available upon starting and refreshes every three years.

**Co-working Spaces** — If you prefer working from a co-working space, the company will reimburse up to $400 per month for a hot desk or dedicated desk membership.

**Team Gatherings** — We hold company-wide offsites twice per year, typically in spring and fall. Each squad also has a budget for one additional in-person gathering per year. Attendance at the company offsite is strongly encouraged but not mandatory.

## Paid Time Off and Benefits

DocuMind offers a flexible PTO policy. We trust team members to take the time they need to rest and recharge. There is no fixed number of PTO days, but we ask that everyone take at least 15 days off per year to avoid burnout.

**Requesting Time Off** — Submit PTO requests through our HR system at least two weeks in advance for planned vacations of three or more days. For shorter absences, notify your squad lead at least two days ahead. Emergency and sick leave do not require advance notice.

**Holidays** — We observe ten company holidays per year aligned with US federal holidays. Team members outside the US may swap these for local holidays with manager approval.

**Parental Leave** — Primary caregivers receive 16 weeks of fully paid leave. Secondary caregivers receive 8 weeks of fully paid leave. Leave can be taken flexibly within the first year of the child's birth or adoption.

**Health Insurance** — The company covers 100% of employee premiums and 75% of dependent premiums for our gold-tier health plan. Dental and vision coverage is included. Coverage begins on your first day of employment.

**401(k) Retirement Plan** — We offer a 401(k) plan with a 4% company match, vesting immediately. Enrollment is automatic at 6% contribution unless you opt out or change your contribution level.

## Code Review Standards

All code changes must be reviewed by at least one other engineer before merging to the main branch. We use pull requests for all changes, no matter how small.

**Review Expectations** — Reviewers should respond to pull requests within one business day. If you cannot review within that timeframe, leave a comment saying so and suggest an alternative reviewer. Reviews should be thorough but constructive. Focus on correctness, clarity, and maintainability rather than style preferences.

**Pull Request Guidelines** — Keep pull requests focused and reasonably sized. A good PR addresses a single concern and can be reviewed in under 30 minutes. Include a clear description of what the change does, why it is necessary, and how to test it. Add screenshots for UI changes.

**Testing Requirements** — All new features must include unit tests. Bug fixes should include a regression test that would have caught the bug. Integration tests are required for API endpoints and database interactions. Aim for meaningful coverage rather than hitting an arbitrary percentage target.

**Merge Process** — After approval, the PR author is responsible for merging their own code. Use squash-and-merge for feature branches to keep the main branch history clean. Delete feature branches after merging.

## Communication Guidelines

Effective communication is the foundation of a successful remote team. We use several tools and follow specific practices to stay aligned.

**Slack** is our primary communication tool. Use public channels for work discussions so information is discoverable. Direct messages are for personal or sensitive topics only. Set your status to indicate your availability.

**Linear** is our project management tool. All work items, bugs, and feature requests are tracked here. Update your assigned issues daily to reflect progress.

**Notion** is our documentation platform. Meeting notes, design documents, technical specifications, and process documentation all live here. When making a decision, document the rationale in Notion so others can understand the context later.

**Email** is reserved for external communication with customers, partners, and vendors. Internal email should be avoided in favor of Slack and Notion.

Weekly all-hands meetings happen every Monday at 11:00 AM Eastern. Squad standups happen daily at times chosen by each squad. Monthly retrospectives review what went well, what could improve, and what actions to take going forward.
